# Website Design Training Playbook

## 1. Concept before components
- Define one creative concept and one intended emotion before choosing layout patterns.
- The concept must come from the business, audience, offer and available evidence; never copy a reference brand.
- Choose one signature visual device that can recur across hero, transitions and supporting sections.
- Prefer one memorable move executed consistently over many fashionable effects.
- If the business has little visual material, make typography, spacing, composition and honest abstract art carry the identity.

## 2. Attention hierarchy
- Every element must earn its visual weight. Primary content dominates; orientation/navigation recedes once understood.
- The first viewport needs one clear focal point, immediate positioning/value and one obvious next action.
- Do not let badges, nav, metadata, secondary CTAs or decorative effects compete with the primary message.
- Use contrast, scale, whitespace and position before adding borders, shadows or color.
- A visitor should understand what matters in roughly one scan without reading every line.

## 3. Grid, alignment and spatial rhythm
- Use a deliberate grid/baseline system; every edge should align intentionally or break alignment for a clear reason.
- Apply optical corrections when perception beats mathematical centering.
- Alternate dense and quiet sections. Do not stack identical full-width bands with the same padding repeatedly.
- Vary composition while preserving a coherent spacing scale.
- Preserve useful line lengths; long copy should not span the full viewport.
- Negative space is structural, not leftover space.

## 4. Typography is architecture
- Pair display and body roles intentionally. Headlines create character; body text optimizes comprehension.
- Use confident scale contrast instead of making every heading medium-large.
- Avoid excessive uppercase, tiny low-contrast text and decorative fonts for functional copy.
- Keep weight, tracking and line-height consistent with the intended emotion.
- Headline wrapping is part of the composition; avoid awkward orphan words and clipped display type.

## 5. Authentic media and proof
- Real supplied work, product, people and place imagery outrank decorative assets.
- Do not fake documentary photography, testimonials, projects, customers or results.
- When proof exists, surface it early and visually: finished work, process, before/after, product UI, verified reviews, team or material detail.
- When proof is absent, be visually honest. Use abstract composition or type rather than synthetic “real-looking” evidence.
- Crop images deliberately. The focal subject should survive desktop and mobile crops.

## 6. Conversion without visual desperation
- One primary action should dominate; secondary actions are quieter.
- Repeat the primary action only where user intent naturally peaks.
- Avoid CTA clutter, multiple competing colors and constant “contact us” blocks.
- The copy around the CTA should reduce uncertainty, not add hype.
- Local-service sites must make phone/contact easy without turning the page into a directory listing.

## 7. Motion and interaction
- Motion must explain hierarchy, state, cause/effect or brand character; decoration alone is insufficient.
- Prefer transform and opacity; never use transition: all.
- Keep interactions interruptible and respect prefers-reduced-motion.
- Hover, focus and active states should increase clarity/contrast.
- Motion density should be low enough that still screenshots remain strong.

## 8. Mobile is a separate composition
- Recompose rather than shrink desktop. Reorder hierarchy when necessary.
- Preserve focal intent, readable line lengths, crop intent and CTA access.
- Interactive targets should be comfortable for touch; aim for at least 44 px on mobile.
- Avoid horizontal overflow, clipped display type and fixed-width decorative elements.
- Navigation should remain understandable without consuming the whole first viewport.

## 9. Anti-template discipline
- Avoid card soup: do not put every fact inside a rounded rectangle.
- Avoid arbitrary radii, decorative glassmorphism, generic gradient blobs and excessive pills.
- Avoid repeated three-column icon/text blocks unless the content genuinely benefits from that comparison.
- Avoid meaningless marquees, logo strips without real logos, fake badges and generic “trusted by” patterns.
- Avoid default SaaS hero composition outside SaaS/product contexts.
- Sections must differ in purpose and rhythm, not only background color.

## 10. Sector adaptation
- Product/technology: product evidence, precision, progressive disclosure and restrained interface chrome.
- Premium/editorial: scale, whitespace, art direction, materiality and fewer stronger elements.
- Hospitality: atmosphere and desire plus practical visit/reservation information.
- Professional services: calm authority, evidence and information hierarchy without corporate-template blandness.
- Craft/local: real work, process, material and locality; avoid stock-worker imagery and trust-icon walls.
- Hair/beauty: editorial texture, movement and personality when appropriate; never default to blush/beige femininity.
- Barber/grooming: craft and culture without moustache/pole/skull/fake-vintage clichés.

## 11. Self-critique before declaring finished
- Ask: could the logo/name be swapped and this still fit ten unrelated businesses? If yes, identity is too generic.
- Ask: what single visual moment will the client remember after closing the page?
- Ask: what element is competing with the primary message without earning that attention?
- Ask: does mobile preserve the concept or merely compress it?
- Ask: is every claim and visual proof honest?
- Ask: would removing an effect improve clarity? If yes, remove it.

## Learned lessons
- Add only lessons supported by repeated training evidence. Deduplicate aggressively.
